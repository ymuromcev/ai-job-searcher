## What this skill gives you

In one sentence: it walks you from "I heard about a call" to "I had it
and learned from it," and remembers everything that came before — cases,
patterns, feedback, your growth curve.

This is not one-click magic. **Chat-based workflow:** you tell Claude you
have an interview; the coach walks you through it command by command and
writes results to files you come back to.

## Workflow for one interview

Every call goes through this cycle. Call commands by speaking — either
`/interview-coach <command>` or natural language ("let's prep for
Synoptix").

| Command | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| `prep` | as soon as you know about the interview | Discovery of company + interviewer + generation of brief (creates `YYYY-MM-DD_*.md`). Before generating, the coach announces the plan and asks for OK (no silent runs). |
| `practice` | 2-4 days before the call | The coach throws a typical question → you answer (text or voice) → it scores on 5 dimensions (Substance, Structure, Relevance, Credibility, Differentiation). Iterate to 4+/5. |
| `mock` | 1-2 days before (optional) | Full interview simulation, 30-45 minutes. Use if you fear "falling apart under live pressure." |
| `hype` | the evening before | Anxiety routine matched to your profile: what to eat / avoid, what not to read, how to sleep, what to do in the morning. Not a motivational speech — a real pre-call protocol. |
| **(the call)** | — | — |
| `debrief` | within 24 hours after the call | What you learned, what you said, what landed / stuck, action items for the next round, score history update. |
| `feedback` | if the recruiter sent feedback | Logs verbatim into Interview Intelligence → influences the next `prep`. |

Additional commands (used less often, situationally):

- `stories` — assemble cases from storybank into battle-ready STAR (needed before a founder/CTO round).
- `concerns` — work through a specific concern ("not enough enterprise-track" → counter strategy).
- `questions` — generate questions for the interviewer for a specific round.
- `progress` — see how you've grown over the last N sessions.
- `analyze` — break down a transcript of a real call (if you have a recording).
- `linkedin` / `resume` / `pitch` / `outreach` — surfaces tangent to the interview itself but feeding the same storybank.
- `decode` — parse a JD before applying.
- `negotiate` / `salary` — when you're at the offer stage.

## What accumulates between interviews (the main value)

After every real call, `debrief` extends the persistent layer:

- **Storybank** — your cases become battle-tested. Example: *"S005 used 3 times, average score 4.2 on Relevance, top story for enterprise-B2B questions."*
- **Score History** — growth curve across 5 dimensions, you see the dynamics.
- **Interview Intelligence** — what works, what doesn't. Example: *"leading with counterintuitive choice scores 4+", "billing migration story stuck below 3 — retire or rework."*
- **Coaching notes** — what the coach remembers about you as a client (anxiety profile, language tendencies, freeze patterns, individual triggers).
- **Calibration state** — how your self-assessment compares to real outcomes (over-rater / under-rater / accurate).

**This is the difference from ad-hoc prep:** every next interview is
cheaper, because 80% of the infrastructure is already there. After 4-5
cycles, you arrive at a new interview with a verified story library and
an accurate map of what actually works for you.

## If you just learned about an interview — what to do

1. Tell Claude: *"I have an interview with [person] from [company] [when]"*.
2. The coach will confirm the plan → then run `prep`.
3. A file `YYYY-MM-DD_<event>.md` will appear in this folder.
4. Read it once — Critical Reframe + Interviewer Intelligence first.
5. 2-4 days out, run `practice` on the 3-5 key questions.
6. The evening before, run `hype`.
7. 15 minutes before the call, read the Day-Of Cheat Sheet only (last section of the brief).
8. After the call, run `debrief` within 24 hours.

The brief itself ends with an adaptive footer **"What you have now and
what to do next"** — that section will tell you exactly what command to
run next, with dates. You don't need to remember the workflow above — the
brief reminds you.

## Principles the skill stands on

- **Quotes in English, instructions in your narrative language.** Everything
  you'll say out loud in the interview is English. Everything else is in
  the language you're comfortable thinking in. See `references/conventions.md`
  rule 1: English quotes are spelled out in full words, no symbols.
- **Headline → number → pause.** Don't try to be eloquent in your second
  language under load. Headline → number → pause. Let the interviewer
  react.
- **Forwardable formulations.** Every answer should contain one form the
  interviewer can repeat to others in their own words later.
- **Honesty over polish.** When caught on a weak spot — a direct answer
  with counter-framing beats deflection.
- **Earned secrets beat buzzwords.** One real insight is worth ten trendy
  words.
- **No fabricated case profiles.** The coach will not invent the
  commercial nature of your past projects from a tag. If a story's
  commercial profile isn't confirmed, the coach asks you. See
  `references/conventions.md` rule 2.
- **Mistakes are reported directly.** If the coach gets a fact wrong or
  ships a broken brief, you'll hear about it in one sentence + a fix
  plan, not a long apology. See `references/conventions.md` rule 3.

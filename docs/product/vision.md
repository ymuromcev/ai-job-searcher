# Vision

## Problem

Generic job search is broken.

The volume is enormous and the signal is thin: most postings are
either off-target, already filled, or reposted aggregator noise. A
serious application requires custom work — a tailored cover letter,
fit research, salary calibration, and follow-up — and that cost is
paid per posting.

So the candidate either applies broadly with low effort and gets
ghosted, or applies narrowly with high effort and runs out of energy
after a dozen attempts. Recruiters ghost regardless of which path the
candidate takes, and the candidate has no system for learning what
worked, what was rejected, and why.

The cost of "a real shot" — researched company, customized cover
letter, calibrated salary ask, scheduled follow-up — is high enough
that doing it manually for every posting is not sustainable.

Most of the work is also repetitive: the same fit narrative, the same
salary band, the same follow-up cadence, regenerated by hand each
time.

## Target user

A *committed* job seeker with a clearly defined target. Specific role
family, specific tier and seniority, specific geography. Not a casual
browser comparing the market.

The user knows the answers to: what role family am I after, what
level, which industries are in scope, which are out, what is my
salary floor, where am I willing to work, what is my schedule.
Without those answers, no automation helps; the engine multiplies
whatever direction it is pointed at.

The user also has a maintainer in the loop — somebody (often the user
themselves) who runs the engine, reviews each draft application, and
presses send. The product is built around that human review step, not
around it.

## Vision

A personal job-search engine that behaves like a senior agency
working a single client.

Concretely it surfaces only relevant postings (filters by role,
level, geography, salary band, company tier), drafts the first 80% of
each application (resume archetype, cover letter, salary research),
tracks every reply (Gmail integration with classifier and Notion
status updates), and learns from rejections (rejection log,
retroactive blocklist sweeps, fit-prerank tuning).

The operator stays in the loop on every send. There is no auto-apply,
no autonomous outreach, no background daemon firing applications
while the operator sleeps. The engine prepares; the operator reviews
and decides.

The flagship surface is Notion — a per-profile Kanban board where
every job is a card with all the context attached. Notion is the UI;
the CLI is the engine; Gmail is the inbox; the operator is the
decision-maker.

## Why multi-profile

One engine, many candidates. Code is shared; data and Notion
workspaces are isolated.

A single maintainer often supports more than one job seeker — a
partner, a family member, a friend. Building a fresh pipeline per
candidate means re-deriving the same architecture, re-debugging the
same Notion quirks, and watching the implementations drift apart.
That was the v1 reality (see
[ADR-001](../architecture/adrs/001-multi-profile.md)).

The multi-profile design treats the profile as data and the engine as
a service. Adding a candidate is a markdown intake form and a deploy
script; the engine code does not change. Filter rules, resume
archetypes, cover letter voice, salary models, Notion hub flavor —
all live under `profiles/<id>/`. Secrets live in one root `.env`,
namespaced per profile.

Two candidates with very different industries (PM-Pete in AI /
fintech, Healthcare-Hannah in regional clinics) run on the same code
path with zero coupling.

## Non-goals

- Not an HR product. The engine optimizes for the candidate, not the
  employer. No applicant-tracking features, no reverse-recruiter
  tooling.
- Not a job board. Postings come from existing ATS feeds (Greenhouse,
  Lever, Ashby, Workday, RemoteOK, USAJOBS, CalCareers) or manual
  research. The engine indexes; it does not host.
- Not a SaaS. Personal toolkit, public source. No hosted multi-tenant
  offering, no signup flow, no billing.
- No auto-apply. The operator presses send. Every cover letter, every
  Notion status change that affects external systems, every recruiter
  reply is reviewed.
- No LinkedIn automation that violates ToS. No scraped résumé
  databases, no fake profile generation, no message blasting.
- No public roadmap or commitments. Stages land when they land; the
  changelog is the record after the fact.

## Success criteria

The engine succeeds when, compared to a baseline of cold applications
via public listings, the operator gets:

- A measurably higher response rate per application sent. Signal:
  rejection vs interview-invite ratios in the rejection log over a
  30-day window.
- A lower time cost per application. Minutes rather than hours,
  because the cover letter draft, salary band, and fit narrative are
  pre-generated.
- Full retention of postings already seen, so a posting is never
  reviewed twice and a previously rejected company is not re-pitched
  without the operator deciding to.
- A pipeline that is auditable: every application has its cover
  letter, resume version, salary band, and Notion timeline preserved,
  and every status change has a recorded cause.

These are not benchmarked publicly. They are the operator's private
metric. The repo ships the tooling; the operator runs the experiment
on themselves.
